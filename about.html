Our History

Eritrea gained independence in 1993 after a long struggle for self-determination. The country's name comes from the Greek word for the Red Sea, Erythra Thalassa, which borders its eastern coast. Today, Eritrea is known for its resilience, diverse traditions, and proud heritage.

Culture and People

Eritrea is home to nine recognised ethnic groups, including the Tigrinya, Tigre, Afar, Saho, and others. Languages such as Tigrinya, Arabic, and English are widely spoken. Traditional music, dance, and cuisine like injera and zigni reflect centuries of African, Arab, and Italian influences.

Key Things to Know Before You Travel

Essential cultural and practical information to help you prepare for your trip to Eritrea.

🗣️

Languages

Tigrinya and Arabic are the most widely spoken languages. English is commonly used in hotels, government offices, and tourism settings. A few basic Tigrinya phrases go a long way with locals.

☕

Coffee Ceremony

The traditional coffee ceremony is a cornerstone of Eritrean hospitality. Expect to be invited for freshly roasted coffee served with popcorn or toasted barley — it's a social ritual that can last an hour or more.

🍽️

Food and Dining

Injera (spongy sourdough flatbread) is the staple, served with stews like zigni (spicy beef or lamb) and tsebhi. Meals are often shared from a large platter, eaten with the right hand. Italian influences mean excellent pasta and espresso are widely available.

👗

Dress Code and Etiquette

Dress modestly, especially when visiting churches, mosques, and government buildings. Shoulders and knees should be covered. Remove shoes before entering homes and places of worship. Greet elders first and use your right hand for eating, greeting, and handing items.

📸

Photography

Always ask permission before photographing people, military sites, police, or government buildings. Photography restrictions are taken seriously.

💵

Currency

The local currency is the Eritrean Nakfa (ERN). Cash is preferred — credit cards are rarely accepted outside major hotels. Bring USD or EUR to exchange at banks or authorised bureaus.

Traditions and Festivals

Eritrea's calendar is rich with religious and cultural celebrations that offer a window into local life.

Orthodox Christian Festivals

Timkat (Epiphany) and Ethiopian Christmas (Leddet) are celebrated with vibrant processions, traditional white clothing, and church services — especially striking in Asmara and the highlands.

Islamic Celebrations

Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are widely observed, particularly in coastal and lowland regions, with community prayers and shared meals.

Independence Day

Celebrated on 24 May with parades, cultural performances, and national pride across the country.
